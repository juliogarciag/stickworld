# 0008 - How things hurt the stick man and how it hurts them: the one who is struck owns the hit test

Status: accepted. Date: 2026-11-27.

## Context

Until issue #43 the only way to die was to fall off the paper, which the `player` feature notices by itself and answers in the same tick by asking for the level again. The club enemy is the first thing that hurts the stick man from outside its own feature, and the spear of #44 is the first thing with which the stick man hurts something else. Three questions had no answer in the code, and each could have been answered in the enemy's folder in a way the next feature would have had to copy or undo:

- Where the test for "did this touch that" lives. It needs the shape of the thing that strikes (the enemy's club, which only the `enemy` feature knows) and the outline of the thing that is struck (the stick man's, which only the `player` feature knows, and no feature may read another's slice, ADR 0007).
- Who decides what a hit does. Being struck has to knock the stick man out of a seat and out of a jump, stop every key, and start the level again after a pause, all of which is the player's state.
- How long a death takes. A fall restarts the level on the tick it is noticed; a blow has to be seen, so the stick man lies there first. That is a state with a duration, which the sim did not have.

## Decision

**The one who strikes says where its weapon is; the one who is struck owns its outline, the test and what being struck does. They meet in one named function in the folder of the one who is struck, and the weapon's shape is a `Blow` from `core/blow.ts`.**

- `core/blow.ts` holds the shared vocabulary and nothing else: `Blow` (a straight line with a thickness and the way it knocks, where a club or a spear is at one instant), `Box` (an outline with its sides along the axes) and `doesBlowTouchBox`. Every hit in the game is that one test, so two features cannot disagree about whether something was touched.
- Hurting the stick man is `strikePlayerWith(sim, blow)` in `features/player/body.ts`, and it is the only way. It tests the blow against the stick man's box (`getPlayerBox`: its width and height, on its feet), and when it touches, the `player` feature does everything that follows from it. It returns whether the blow struck, so the one who strikes can stop looking. It refuses a stick man that is struck down already and one under a flag it has reached.
- A swing is many blows. The one who strikes calls `strikePlayerWith` for every place its weapon passed through in the tick (the club is tested at `stepsPerTick` places along the tick's part of its arc), because the end of a club moves most of a stick man's width in one tick and must not step over it. Which part of a movement hurts is the striker's to say: the enemy shows the club only during the swing, never while it goes back or is lifted again, and never its body.
- Being struck is a state of the player, not an event: `ticksSinceStruck` and `struckDirection` in its slice. Struck, no command has any effect on the stick man (a walk or a jump is still heard and then ignored by its `update`; the slice is new when the level starts again), falls to the floor if it was in the air, and after `PLAYER.struckPause` the player asks for the level again with `startLevelAfterThisTick`, exactly as a fall does. So everything a death already means holds without a new rule: what is in a slot is kept, what is in the world is lost, the letter's retry gives the airplane back. `isPlayerStruck(sim)` is what the other features ask: the two action keys answer nothing, a flag is not reached, a carried airplane is let go of, a letter is put away.
- A solid body is separate from a blow. Touching the enemy does not hurt, so its body never goes through `strikePlayerWith`; it goes through `keepPlayerOutOfBox(sim, box)`, also in `body.ts`, which moves the stick man out sideways using the same outline the end of a floor line pushes (a point at its feet, its full width higher up). That is why a jump clears a body and a walk does not, with no rule about jumping in the enemy.
- The other direction is the same decision turned round, and is what #44 builds: the feature that can be struck exports the function, and the weapon's owner calls it with a `Blow`. For the enemies that is a function in `features/enemy` (say `strikeEnemiesWith(sim, blow)`), which tests the blow against each enemy's box (`getEnemyBox` in `features/enemy/guard.ts`, already there for every enemy's solid body) and decides what a struck enemy does (it is rubbed out). The spear's feature works out where the spear is each tick of a thrust and calls it, as the enemy does with its club. Nothing in `core/blow.ts` or in the player changes for it.
- Feature order: `enemy` comes after everything that moves the stick man (`player`, and `airplane`, which moves its seat), so a body and a club meet the stick man where it is at the end of the tick. A feature whose weapon the stick man carries comes after the player for the same reason, and before `enemy`: when a thrust and a swing land on the same tick the thrust is first, the enemy is rubbed out and its club strikes nothing. A tie goes to the player.

## What was considered and not done

- A hit test in the striker's folder, reading the stick man's position through `getPlayerBody` and its size from `content/tuning.ts`. It works for one enemy; the second would copy the outline, and the day the stick man's outline changes (crouching, a shield) every copy is wrong without a failing test.
- A `combat` feature that knows every weapon and every body, the way `item` and `world` own a key (ADR 0003). Those two exist because one press must mean one thing among several features' answers. A blow has no such contest: it is tested against everything it could touch. A feature in the middle would only forward calls and would have to import every other feature.
- Damage and health. Nothing in the game takes two hits: the stick man is struck down or not, an enemy is rubbed out or not. `strikePlayerWith` takes a `Blow` and no amount; the day something needs an amount it is a field on `Blow`, which is why the type is shared.
- A death as a `'player/die'` command. Commands are what the player means (rule 2); nothing the sim does to itself is one.
- Starting the level again on the tick of the hit, like a fall, and leaving the fall to the view. The view would have had to keep drawing a level the sim had already left, which is what the page turn does with a second sim (ADR 0005); a death is not a page turn, and the enemy has to be seen finishing its swing.

## Consequences

- A new way to hurt the stick man is a feature that works out where its weapon is and calls `strikePlayerWith`. It needs no change in the player and no knowledge of the stick man's size.
- A new thing that can be hurt exports its own `strike…With` and owns its outline. A weapon's owner calls one function per kind of thing it can hurt; when there are several kinds, that list in the weapon's feature is the place to look for what a weapon can touch.
- `doesBlowTouchBox` grows the box by half the blow's thickness on every side, so a corner is touched from a little further than a round end would reach (about a fifth of the thickness), and a blow reaches half its thickness past its end. Where the club is while it goes back and is lifted again hurts nobody and is in the sim all the same: the club has one place, and the view draws that. Outlines are boxes: the stick man's limbs and an enemy's peaked top are view detail (rule 8) and the view draws inside the box.
- The sim has a second place with trigonometry (`Math.sin` and `Math.cos` in `features/enemy/clubEnemy`, for where the club is on its arc), with the consequence AGENTS.md already states for the glide: exact replay within one engine, which is what the tests and a restored snapshot need.
- The pause of a struck death is the first time the stick man is on the paper and deaf to the keys other than under a flag. A feature that gives a key a meaning asks `isPlayerStruck` where it already asks `isFlagReached`, or goes through `item` and `world`, which ask for it.
- `features/enemy` holds what every enemy has (its post, its solid body, the stretch it guards, being struck) and one folder per kind for what that kind does: `clubEnemy/` has the club enemy's def, its rules and its part of the view. A second kind is a folder next to it, a line in the feature's `update`, a member of the `EnemyView` union and a drawing in the client; nothing of the club enemy is renamed for it.
- The enemy is the first feature that acts with no command. It is still a plain function of the sim's state each tick, with no rng, so the determinism rule holds as it is; an enemy that should surprise draws from `sim.rng`.
